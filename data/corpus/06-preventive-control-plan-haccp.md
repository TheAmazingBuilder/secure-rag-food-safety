Title: Preventive control plans and HACCP

Under the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations, most food businesses must prepare, implement, keep, and maintain a written preventive control plan, or PCP. A PCP is a written document that shows how hazards and risks to food are eliminated or effectively controlled.

The Food Safety Enhancement Program, or FSEP, is one example of a type of preventive control plan. It is based on Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point, or HACCP, principles, and it has long been used by meat, dairy, processed fruit and vegetable, egg, honey, and maple operators, as well as hatcheries.

A PCP describes the control measures that prevent, eliminate, or reduce to an acceptable level the hazards identified as presenting a risk of contamination, together with evidence of their effectiveness. It includes a hazard analysis, preventive controls, critical control points with critical limits, monitoring, corrective actions, verification, and record keeping.

Businesses with a plan developed before the Safe Food for Canadians Regulations should review it against the preventive control requirements of those regulations and update it as needed.
