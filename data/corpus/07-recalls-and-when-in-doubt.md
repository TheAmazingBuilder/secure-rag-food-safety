Title: Recalls and when in doubt
Source: https://healthycanadians.gc.ca/eating-nutrition/safety-salubrite/tips-conseils/food-steps-precautions-aliments-eng.php
Accessed: 2026-11-11
Status: Summary — paraphrased from the source, not a verbatim quote.

You cannot tell if food is unsafe by its smell or taste. When in doubt, throw it out. This applies to food that may have been left in the temperature danger zone, stored for too long, or handled in a way that could have caused contamination.

The Government of Canada publishes food recall notices when a food may present a health risk, for example because of undeclared allergens or contamination. A recall notice identifies the affected product, the reason for the recall, and what consumers should do, which is usually to not eat the product and to return it or throw it away.

Check recall notices before eating a product you are unsure about, and follow the instructions in the notice. If you think you have a foodborne illness, see a health care professional and contact your local public health unit as soon as possible.
