Title: Safe internal cooking temperatures
Source: https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/hc-sc/documents/services/general-food-safety-tips/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures/safe-internal-cooking-temperatures.pdf
Accessed: 2026-11-11
Status: Summary — paraphrased from the source, not a verbatim quote.

Health Canada publishes a safe internal cooking temperatures chart. Temperatures should be measured with a food thermometer at the thickest part of the food.

Ground beef, veal, lamb, and pork should reach 71 degrees Celsius (160 degrees Fahrenheit). Ground poultry should reach 74 degrees Celsius (165 degrees Fahrenheit).

Beef, veal, and lamb pieces and whole cuts: medium-rare is 63 degrees Celsius (145 degrees Fahrenheit), medium is 71 degrees Celsius (160 degrees Fahrenheit), and well done is 77 degrees Celsius (170 degrees Fahrenheit). Pork pieces and whole cuts should reach 71 degrees Celsius (160 degrees Fahrenheit).

Poultry pieces should reach 74 degrees Celsius (165 degrees Fahrenheit), and whole poultry should reach 82 degrees Celsius (180 degrees Fahrenheit). Frozen raw breaded chicken products should reach 74 degrees Celsius (165 degrees Fahrenheit).

Leftovers, egg dishes, and stuffing cooked alone or in poultry should reach 74 degrees Celsius (165 degrees Fahrenheit). Cooked food is safe only when its internal temperature has reached the recommended value; colour alone is not a reliable sign.
