Title: Food allergen labelling in Canada
Source: https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-labels/labelling/consumers/food-allergies
Accessed: 2026-11-11
Status: Summary — paraphrased from the source, not a verbatim quote.

In Canada, the most common food allergens are known as priority allergens and must be clearly identified on prepackaged food labels. The priority allergens are eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and molluscs, fish, sesame seeds, soy, sulphites, tree nuts, and wheat and triticale.

Gluten sources must also be identified on prepackaged food labels. The grains that are gluten sources include barley, oats, rye, triticale, and wheat.

Prepackaged food products that contain priority food allergens and gluten sources must include them in the list of ingredients and/or in a statement that begins with "Contains" on their label. Allergens must be declared by their prescribed source names.

Cross-contamination statements, such as "may contain", must not be used when an allergen, allergen-containing ingredient, or gluten source is intentionally added to a food. When the Canadian Food Inspection Agency becomes aware of a potential serious hazard, such as undeclared allergens, it investigates and may request a recall of the food product.
