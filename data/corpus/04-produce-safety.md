Title: Produce safety
Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/general-food-safety-tips/produce-safety.html
Accessed: 2026-11-11
Status: Summary — paraphrased from the source, not a verbatim quote.

Fresh vegetables and fruits do not naturally contain the microorganisms that cause food poisoning, but they can become contaminated during growing, handling, or preparation. Because much fresh produce is eaten without cooking, safe handling matters.

Buy cold or frozen food at the end of a shopping trip. Examine vegetables and fruits and avoid items that are bruised or damaged. Pre-cut or ready-to-eat vegetables and fruit should be properly refrigerated at 4 degrees Celsius (40 degrees Fahrenheit) or below.

Wash hands with soap and warm water for at least 20 seconds before handling produce. Wash fresh fruit and vegetables with potable water before use, and cut away bruised or damaged areas, since harmful bacteria can gather there. Use a vegetable brush on produce with a firm skin, such as carrots and melons.

Refrigerate the vegetables and fresh fruits that need refrigeration. Pregnant women, children under the age of 5, adults over the age of 60, and people with weakened immune systems are at higher risk of serious effects from foodborne illness and should take extra care.
