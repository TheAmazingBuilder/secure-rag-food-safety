Title: Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill — the four core steps
Source: https://healthycanadians.gc.ca/eating-nutrition/safety-salubrite/tips-conseils/food-steps-precautions-aliments-eng.php
Accessed: 2026-11-11
Status: Summary — paraphrased from the source, not a verbatim quote.

Health Canada and the Canadian Food Inspection Agency promote four core steps to prevent foodborne illness: clean, separate, cook, and chill.

Clean means washing hands with soap and warm water for at least 20 seconds, especially before and after handling raw meat, poultry, fish, or seafood. It also means washing surfaces, utensils, and cutting boards, and rinsing fresh fruit and vegetables under running water.

Separate means keeping raw meat, poultry, fish, and seafood away from cooked food and ready-to-eat food. Use one cutting board for produce and a different one for raw meat, poultry, fish, and seafood, and clean and sanitize boards and utensils between uses.

Cook means cooking food to a safe internal temperature, checked with a clean food thermometer rather than by colour or appearance. Bacteria such as E. coli, Salmonella, and Listeria are killed by heat when food reaches the right temperature.

Chill means keeping cold food cold and hot food hot. Refrigerate or freeze perishable food promptly, within two hours, and never let food sit in the temperature danger zone longer than needed.
